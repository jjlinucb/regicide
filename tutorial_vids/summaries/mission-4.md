# Mission 4 — Fusion of Darkness

## Story
A trusted ally reveals his true colors, and the party has to defend something called "the evergreen" from a wave of monstrous beasts.

## How it plays / special rules
Confirmed by a full solo playthrough (Meet Me at the Table, "Mission 4 Playthrough" — three attempts, the third a win):

- Standard 4-4-4 escalating lineup: four enemies at 10 strength/20 health, four at 15/30, four at 20/40 (matches this codebase's tier stats exactly).
- **Discard-pile buff:** whatever card currently sits on top of the discard pile adds its value straight onto the active enemy's attack. This is snapshotted once per turn — you take the buffed damage, choose which cards to discard to cover it in one action, and only *after* those cards land does the new top card apply to the *next* enemy. It doesn't retroactively change the damage you're currently paying down.
- **Exact kills** go to the top of the reserve deck instead of the discard pile; any other kill's played cards go to the discard pile as normal (feeding the buff above).
- **Corrupted return queue:** a defeated enemy isn't gone — it rejoins the back of the 12-enemy fight queue once, corrupted (ignores immunity, its own play banishes the reserve deck's top card), then stays gone for good if defeated again. The video calls this out up front as a general warning before the fight starts, not something that hits an enemy's first appearance.
- The playthrough confirms this is one of the hardest missions in the campaign — the host lost twice before winning on the third attempt, specifically because of how the discard-buff and corrupted-requeue mechanics compound over a long fight.
- Cosmetic note: the physical cards name these enemies "Subject N, Phase I/II/III Experiment" rather than this codebase's invented "Specimen NN-Letter: The Whatever" flavor names — stats line up exactly, only the names differ, so no code change implied.

## Rewards (two relics/rewards this mission)
- **Gøran joins the party** — introduced in this mission's own ending, as a basic rank-8 card (no suit revealed yet, described as getting "much cooler later").
- **Beast companions (x4):** tied to a specific character, these work like Animal Companions — paired with one other card — but instead of adding a flat +1 strength, a beast *copies* the strength of the card it's paired with (worth 0 alone). The video's own framing of the reward is "keep the four beasts in a box next to your party; each mission attempt you may include *one* of the four in your reserve deck" — i.e. a per-attempt pick from a shared pool of four, not all four merged permanently into the party's decks at once.
- **Relic — Scarlet Whistle:** opens whenever a player attacks alone with a lone Animal/Beast Companion. In co-op, any other player may silently add one card from their hand to the attack before it resolves. The video separately confirms a **solo-specific variant** the host called out as something not commonly shown: in solo play, the lone attacker instead pulls the top card of the discard pile and folds its value into the attack.

### Implemented
1. **Scarlet Whistle's solo variant.** `engine.ts`'s `playCards` previously only opened the assist window multiplayer (`state.players.length > 1`); solo just resolved the lone Companion with no bonus at all. Now, solo play with the relic active pulls the top card of the discard pile straight into the attack instead (a no-op if the discard pile is empty, or if its top happens to be a Jester, which can never join a normal combo) — mirrors the Kinfolk Flute's existing solo/co-op split from Mission 1's relic. See `legacy: mission 4 Beast Companions...` describe block in `legacy.test.ts` for the new solo-variant tests.
2. **Gøran now joins here, not (only) at Mission 8.** A comment already in `missions.ts` (near Mission 8's entry) recorded that an earlier research pass had found this exact gap, but by the time it was found, Mission 4 had already shipped without him, so his recruitment was deliberately deferred to Mission 8 instead as a stopgap — Mission 9's Evergreen-upgrade-by-name matching was already built to key off his NAME rather than a specific mission's placement, so moving him didn't require touching that. Mission 8 no longer recruits him. His suit/class (Spades/Paladin) carries over unchanged — still an unsourced judgment call, since no source pins one for his actual first recruitment either way. **Still open:** the source's own later steps — Mission 7 adding him a Hearts suit, and Mission 8 (once he's already in the party) adding a second, Diamonds suit — remain unimplemented; fixing those means reopening Mission 7's own reward, out of scope for this pass.
3. **Beast Companion reward is now a rotating pool, not four permanent recruits.** The 4 beast-flagged recruits granted by this mission's reward no longer go into the persisted campaign party at all — `RoomManager.grantMissionReward` splits them out before `applyReward` ever sees them, into a new `legacy.beastCompanionPool` (persisted separately, a new `beast_companion_pool`/`selected_beast_companion_id` pair of columns on the `campaigns` table). The host picks one (or none) from a new "Beast Companion" panel in the campaign lobby (mirrors the existing Mercenary Camp's shape, but sticky across missions rather than reset per loss streak) via a new `legacy:setBeastCompanionSelection` socket event; `RoomManager.startLegacyMission` folds just that one card in as an extra reserve card for the next attempt — same "not a permanent party member" treatment Mercenaries already get. Mission 11's own `beastDeckMechanic` is unaffected: it still pulls the WHOLE pool into play at once for its finale mechanic, overriding the "pick one" restriction, since `buildBeastDeck` needs all 4 simultaneously and that's the one place the sourced material doesn't describe a per-attempt pick either.
